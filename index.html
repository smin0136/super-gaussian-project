Abstract

Despite the promise of virtual reality (VR) for intuitive spatial interaction, volume visualization (VolVis) in VR remains constrained by high rendering costs and motion discomfort. Recent advances have shown that representing volumetric scenes with 3D Gaussian splatting enables high-performance rendering, mitigating the computational latency typically associated with immersive 3D data exploration, thus making this representation well-suited for VR. However, existing Gaussian-based scene editing workflows remain limited by slow offline segmentation and fatigue-inducing manual selection.

To address these challenges, we present Super-Gaussian, a novel VolVis framework that enhances scene editing and interaction in VR through intuitive 3D Gaussian selection and natural language interaction (NLI). Our approach groups Gaussian primitives into higher-level units via feature-aware clustering, enabling efficient selection of complex volumetric regions, such as tumors in medical images or filaments in cosmological data, without tedious point-by-point interaction.

Building on this representation, we introduce a hierarchical select-and-refine workflow that combines random-walk-based region propagation, cluster selection, and point refinement, allowing users to progressively specify regions of interest with reduced effort. We further support on-the-fly text labeling of user-selected regions using NLI, allowing users to semantically query, interpret, and manipulate selected content within a visualization-perception-action loop.

Overview

The main capabilities emphasized in the current submission.

Structure-aware ROI selection

Super-Gaussian clusters Gaussian splats into semantically meaningful interaction units, then combines random walk, cluster selection, and point refinement for a coarse-to-fine workflow.

NLI inside VR

Users can label, query, and edit selected regions through speech-driven natural language interaction while continuing direct spatial manipulation in the headset.

Editable Gaussian rendering

The framework extends editable Gaussian-based volume visualization to immersive VR, supporting real-time color, opacity, lighting, and viewpoint changes.

User Study

This section preserves the study details that are useful for the project page even if the final paper trims them for space.

Study scope

Two studies were conducted: one focused on framework usability in VR, and the other compared the interactive selection method against existing Gaussian-based baselines.

11 participants 22-31 years old Meta Quest 3 HMD RTX 3090 desktop setup

Hypotheses

  • H1: Super-Gaussian improves accuracy, preference, and fatigue over 2D and point-based selection.
  • H2: The select-and-refine workflow reduces workload through coarse-to-fine selection.
  • H3: Selection plus text labeling and NLI improves understanding of unknown structures.
  • H4: VR exploration improves understanding, selection, and editing of volumetric structures.
Study 1

Framework usability

Participants used the framework on the chameleon, aneurysm, and Nyx datasets after a 15-35 minute training session covering VR manipulation, brush interaction, text labeling, and NLI.

Selection usability 6.20 / 7 Exploration support 6.44 / 7 Spatial understanding 6.60 / 7 NLI interaction 6.03 / 7 Overall experience 6.60 / 7

The reported takeaway is that the framework supports intuitive interaction, effective refinement, and stronger understanding of volumetric data, supporting H3 and H4.

Study 2

Interactive selection comparison

Selection was compared against SuperSplat (2D screen-space) and GSVR (manual point-based VR) on the vortex, lobster, and ionization datasets.

Metrics: ACC, MCC, F1, IoU, precision Effort: interaction count, completion time Workload: NASA-TLX

Super-Gaussian achieved the strongest overall balance of accuracy, workload, and efficiency. The paper reports substantially lower mental demand, temporal demand, effort, and frustration, with the highest perceived performance score (6.60).

Interpretation

What the results suggest

  • Super-Gaussian consistently reduces interaction count and completion time, with the largest gains on vortex and ionization.
  • VR-based selection shows a clear advantage on thin or spatially subtle structures such as the lobster dataset.
  • Participants naturally adopted the intended coarse-to-fine workflow: broad selection first, then localized refinement.
Interview summary

Qualitative feedback

  • Participants reported stronger depth perception and spatial understanding in VR than in 2D tools.
  • NLI helped with understanding unfamiliar structures, though the paper notes that latency and readability could still improve.
  • Suggested future refinements included clearer tool feedback, better undo support, and more intuitive controller mappings.
